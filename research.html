Research

Research & projects

I study how public lands policy gets implemented, and what that means for the agencies doing the work and the rural communities nearby. My methods include qualitative case studies, administrative data analysis, spatial analysis and scenario modeling.

Current projects

2026–2028 · National Science Foundation

Evidence-based policymaking for public lands counties

An NSF EPSCoR Research Fellowship to obtain data science expertise that supports evidence-based policymaking for public lands counties, building on my work on county payments and the fiscal effects of public lands.

2025–2026 · Rural Voices for Conservation Coalition

Evolution in Stewardship Contracting: The “A to Z” model

Documents the purpose, design, community benefits and lessons of the A to Z stewardship contracting model on the Colville National Forest. The project looks at how the mechanism can reflect place-based community values while advancing Forest Service priorities. The output is a practitioner resource guide.

MFCN 2025 talkGet in touch
2024–2025 · Resources Legacy Fund

Evaluating the business and policy environment for increasing biomass utilization in the Intermountain West

Surveys a diverse set of biomass business managers (community-scale/traditional and operation/closed/start-up) in the Intermountain West, with a focus on policy barriers and opportunities. Results are documented in a synthesis of themes with actionable recommendations ranging from formal policy change to administrative encumbrances and program modifications.

Impact and outcomes of collaborative national forest governance

A replicable method for measuring how collaborative governance affects national forest management outcomes, using Forest Service administrative data, paired with qualitative work on how agency staff shape collaboration.

Public lands counties: policy reform and scenario planning

An analysis of county payment programs (PILT, Secure Rural Schools) with scenarios showing how different management alternatives would change payments to counties in Idaho and Montana.

Monitoring the Collaborative Forest Landscape Restoration Program

Social and economic monitoring for the Southwestern Crown of the Continent (MT) and Northeast Washington Forest Vision 2020 projects, including subcontracting trends, local contractor participation and benefits to local communities over ten years.

2012–2024 · UM Bureau of Business and Economic Research

Timber-processing capacity near national forests

Documents wood products manufacturers' capacity to use timber of different sizes. Ecological conditions and policy priorities are changing the species, size and quality of timber removed, and this information helps federal land managers design projects that suit local purchasers.
